# Design: Excess Returns to R&D-Intensive Firms

## Frozen scope

This attempt implements the single retained baseline, Section 5 Equation (1), on R&D firm-year observations from 1979–1998. The dependent variable is first-year excess return; the regressors are annual within-year five-group ranks of the change in R&D assets, the R&D asset level, and adjusted return on net operating assets. The Eq. (1) coefficient estimates are pooled, not separate year fits. I do not build the later forecast-error version, the Table 9 five-observation group construction, or other portfolio summaries.

The original paper reports no regression-specific N, estimator, or covariance method for Eq. (1). I use pooled OLS with conventional standard errors as a best-effort estimator and report two-sided p-values in the required exports. Published parent counts (89,419 main firm-years and 35,841 R&D firm-years) are comparison benchmarks, not fixed sample sizes.

## Source and mappings

The original paper controls all inherited extraction choices. Physical PDF page 4 (printed 136) describes the main Compustat annual sample. Physical page 5 (printed 137) defines the five-year R&D asset and amortization. Physical pages 7–8 describe the first-year excess-return construction and 25 size/book-to-market controls. Physical page 16 (printed 148) defines the Table 8 adjusted return on net operating assets; physical page 18 (printed 150) defines the Table 9 change in R&D assets; physical page 19 (printed 151) gives Eq. (1) and its rank directions. Notes 3–7 on physical pages 23–24 (printed 155–156) give the minimum sample fields, missing-XRD rule, benchmark formation and delisting treatment. Notes 16–17 on physical page 25 (printed 157) clarify the return window and the conflicting ARNOA denominator date.

The source field mapping uses current local `COMP.FUNDA`, Xpressfeed currency tables `COMP.G_EXRT_MTH` and `COMP.G_EXRT_DLY`, CRSP `MSF`, `DSI`, `MSENAMES`, and `MSEDELIST`, and dated `CCMXPF_LINKTABLE`. All sources are read-only. The analysis sample uses domestic standard consolidated annual rows (`INDL`, `STD`, `C`, `D`) over 1974–1999 accounting support, with fiscal-year labels 1979–1998 for the sample. `INDL` is the provider's universal core format, not a financial-industry exclusion; no SIC, exchange, share-price, or finance screen is added. The current-vintage local data do not establish original PST/full-coverage/merged-research membership.

Compustat reported R&D expense is `XRD`. Per note 4, missing or immaterial XRD on an observed annual statement is zero; an absent fiscal-year row is not zero and five-year history terms require the corresponding annual rows. R&D asset is the source-weighted sum of current and four prior XRD values; R&D amortization is 0.2 times the five prior-year XRD values. I convert nonzero monetary inputs to USD using Xpressfeed's GBP primary-currency cross-rate formula: `GBP→USD / GBP→CURCD`. Annual flows (`XRD`, `IB`) use `EXRAT12M` matched to the statement's reporting month; balance-sheet amounts use `EXRATD` at the statement date. For a statement date without a same-day daily quote, I use the latest same-date pair of positive GBP legs on or before the statement date, bounded to seven days before it; no future quote is used. The quote date and each leg's actual/carried-forward type are retained independently, with no actual-only or equal-type restriction. USD inputs use a direct factor of one. A source-authorized zero XRD remains zero without a quote; unsupported nonzero translations remain unavailable. This keeps each year's XRD, R&D asset, NOA, adjusted earnings, and book adjustment in USD units comparable with CRSP market equity. The local monthly rate series begins in 1982, so unsupported earlier nonzero foreign-currency flows remain unavailable rather than being backfilled.

For net operating assets I use the cited Nissim–Penman decomposition from *Ratio Analysis and Equity Valuation* (local copy `papers/pdf/W1513538699.pdf`, appendix physical page 41): `DLC + DLTT + PSTK - CHE - IVAO + CEQ + MIB`. Provider documentation identifies legacy item A130 as `PSTK` (Research Insight 2004, physical pages 166 and 219). This is the algebraically simplified version of the cited formula; the preferred treasury-stock/arrears terms cancel. Required terms remain observed, and signed NOA values are retained. NOA must be nonzero only where it is used as a divisor; there is no positive-NOA screen. The cited paper supports the construct only; its sample screens and missing-value rules are not imported.

Adjusted earnings use Compustat `IB` (income before extraordinary items) as the best available mapping of the paper's reported income/earnings language. The paper does not identify a raw earnings mnemonic; `IB` is therefore an explicit best-effort choice. Adjusted earnings add USD-converted XRD and subtract the five-year R&D amortization. Adjusted NOA adds the same-period R&D asset to source-defined NOA.

## Sample, timing, and ranks

The sample anchor is the Compustat fiscal-year observation `t` (1979–1998). The R&D-firm flag is positive five-year `RDASSET_t`. The source's stated main-sample requirements are applied to fiscal year `t`: month of fiscal year end, observed assets, sales, earnings (`IB`), common dividends (`DVC`), common stockholders' equity (`CEQ`), CRSP company market equity at fiscal year end and at the end of month four after fiscal year end, a full 12-month fiscal period, positive year-end book equity, and some stock return availability in the following 12-month window. No extra financial-firm, NYSE-sample, $5-price, or IPO screen is applied.

The outcome window follows note 16: the 12 monthly periods from month five after fiscal year-end `t` through month four after fiscal year-end `t+1`. For a December fiscal year end this is May of `t+1` through April of `t+2`; fiscal-year 1998 windows can extend into 2000. Monthly periods are matched by calendar month, not by equality to calendar month-end. Observed monthly returns are compounded. In a delisting month, observed CRSP RET and DLRET are combined when both exist. A unique DLRET is retained as a return-only event row when the PERMNO has no common-stock MSF row that month. After delisting, the assigned control group's return substitutes for missing target returns through the remainder of that 12-month measurement window. If the window crosses July and the delisted target has no fresh formation record, I carry its last available size/book-to-market cell into the subsequent formation year; this continuation is an operational reading of note 7, which does not spell out the cross-July case. A missing live-firm return, incomplete return window, or unavailable portfolio return remains missing; no ordinary zero-fill is used.

RDA is `RDASSET_t / MVE_t`, using company market equity at the end of month four after fiscal year end. Delta RDA is `(RDASSET_(t+1) - RDASSET_t) / NOA_(t+1)`. Table 9 calls for a net-operating-assets denominator but does not specify its year; I use `t+1` to align the asset change and the year-`t+1` performance measure. ARNOA is adjusted earnings in `t+1` divided by adjusted NOA in `t+1`, following Table 8 physical page 16. Appendix note 17 on physical page 25 instead says NOA at `t`; this unresolved source conflict is recorded, and Table 8 is used because it directly defines the Table 9 performance control. These signed ratios are defined only for nonzero denominators.

Within each sample year, the three underlying variables are independently ranked into five approximately equal groups, then divided by four. Delta RDA is sorted descending, producing a rank that decreases as Delta RDA increases. RDA and ARNOA are sorted ascending. I use the rank group values 0–4, divided by four, as Eq. (1) describes; I do not use Table 9's ARNOA-first groups of five observations.

## Benchmark portfolios and inference

The 25 control portfolios are rebuilt from the local Compustat/CRSP overlap rather than imported standard Fama–French series. For formation year `y`, the accounting observation is selected by the actual calendar year of `DATADATE`: the year-end statement must fall in `y-1`. Where a GVKEY has more than one annual row in that prior calendar year, the full-year row is preferred, then the latest statement date is used; duplicate counts are reported. Size uses company market equity at December of `y-1`; book-to-market uses adjusted book (`CEQ + TXDITC + RDASSET`) and market equity at the statement date in `y-1`; June 30 of `y` links and NYSE membership determine independent quintile breakpoints. A May 1999 statement therefore maps to formation year 2000, with December 1999 size, June 2000 breakpoints, and July 2000–June 2001 returns. No annual formation uses `FYEAR+1` in place of the actual statement calendar year. The sample return windows end by 2000, but the 2000 benchmark support is retained through June 2001.

I use `TXDITC` for the deferred-tax book adjustment because the source says this term follows Fama and French (1993), whose standard item includes deferred taxes and investment tax credit. The local file also has `TXDB` labelled “Deferred Taxes (Balance Sheet),” which more literally matches the paper's “deferred tax liability” wording; the source does not identify a mnemonic. `TXDITC` is the explicit conventional mapping choice. Missing `TXDITC` is not imputed to zero. The source does not name CRSP share codes; I include share types 10 and 11 as ordinary common shares based on the first-digit definition in the CRSP Stock and Index Data Descriptions Guide, physical page 115 (printed 111).

The monthly company market-equity sum is formed from all CRSP issues with share code 10 or 11 active in `MSENAMES` on the final CRSP trading date in `DSI`. Each expected issue must have one monthly MSF record, observed price/share market value, and an agreeing PERMCO in `MSF` and `MSENAMES`; otherwise that company's market equity is unavailable rather than a partial sum. Conflicting name-history PERMCOs mark each possible company incomplete. Any common issue with no attributable PERMCO is reported as an aggregate mapping limitation rather than used to invalidate unrelated companies. CRSP issue market equity is converted from thousands to Compustat millions by dividing by 1,000. CCM dates are applied at statement dates and June 30, including open-ended records. Monthly portfolio returns are value-weighted using prior-month issue market equity and current-month CRSP returns, with all common issues of a company assigned to the company's portfolio.

Formation year 2000 uses 1999 annual accounting, December 1999 market equity, and June 2000 NYSE breakpoints. The complete July 2000–June 2001 benchmark return year is retained; the sample's fiscal-year 1998 return windows end by 2000.

For the sample firm's return, the dated CCM primary security is used. The source does not specify security selection when a company has multiple traded issues; the primary-issue mapping and company-level portfolio weights are explicit implementation choices. Physical pages 6–7 distinguish target matching from benchmark-constituent formation: at the investment date, I match the target's current size and book-to-market to the cutoffs from the preceding June 30, then update the target match on each subsequent June 30. For the initial match, current size is the target company's complete MVE at the end of month four after fiscal year-end, and current book-to-market is `(CEQ + TXDITC + RDASSET)` from that fiscal-year statement divided by that MVE. Thus the initial target match uses the statement for the sample observation, not the lagged accounting row used to build benchmark constituents.

For a June 30 reassignment while the target remains listed, current size is the target company's complete June market equity. Current book-to-market uses the latest supported annual statement for that GVKEY whose assumed availability date has arrived by June 30, with adjusted book `(CEQ + TXDITC + RDASSET)` divided by June market equity. The source does not provide statement release dates or a separate formula for refreshed target book-to-market. I therefore treat a statement as available on the first day of the fifth month after its `DATADATE`, following the paper's investment-date convention; I select the latest such statement available by June 30 and compare it with that June's cutoffs. The selected statement date, June market-equity date, and cutoff year are retained in construction diagnostics. This four-month publication-delay rule is a disclosed timing approximation, not a source-reported filing date. The monthly July–June benchmark constituent assignments and value-weighted returns remain formed under footnote 5. At month-end return assignment, the target group switches for July returns after a June 30 update. If a target delists before that update, I carry the last target assignment available by the delisting month and do not form a later match for it.

## Output and interpretation

`data/rd_main.sas7bdat` is the only sample handoff and contains one row per GVKEY/fiscal year, sample variables, rank variables, annual return-window diagnostics, and the required regression fields. The notebook estimates Eq. (1) with pooled OLS and exports the regression and the actual model-sample descriptives in four formats each. The reported coefficient/t comparison is in the execution review; no target counts or estimates are hard-coded into the data construction.
