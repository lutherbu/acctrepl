# Independent source and code review — W1987881672, attempt 02

## Scope and conclusion

I independently checked all nine Table 1 trend scopes against the paper PDF, frozen model/sample records, the I/B/E/S Detail History guide, predecessor SAS/Stata code, native logs, and exported results. The paper defines one annual outcome for each year 1984–1999, built from quarterly surprise events (physical pp. 5–6 / printed pp. 225–226); Table 1 and its note are on physical p. 10 / printed p. 230. The nine fits all execute on 16 annual observations. Firm-quarter population counts are a separate quantity.

The inherited construction was close to the paper's stated outcomes and strict forecast date rule. I repaired a real unit incompatibility: 47 selected rows had `report_curr='USD'` but `CURR_ACT='CAD'`, so their forecast and actual values were not demonstrably in the same denomination. The revised build excludes those rows after selecting the latest eligible forecast, retains `CURRFL='C'` when the stored report and actual currencies are USD, and rebuilds from the exact catalog source. It does not use published sample counts or estimates as construction targets.

## Source and field checks

- The paper requires a quarterly report date and at least one individual forecast made before it, then uses the single most recent individual forecast rather than consensus or one named analyst (physical pp. 5–6 / printed pp. 225–226). Footnote 7 excludes forecasts dated after the quarter's earnings number is known (physical p. 6 / printed p. 226). The code uses `ANNDATS_ACT` as the explicit actual announce-date field and admits only `ANNDATS < ANNDATS_ACT`; `ANNTIMS` ranks forecasts only after the strict date screen. Same-day forecasts remain excluded even if intraday time is present.
- The source is the catalog-listed `IBES.DET_EPSUS` file. `TICKER` is the persistent I/B/E/S key; `FPEDATS` is the fiscal-period end date; `MEASURE='EPS'` and `USFIRM=1` identify the U.S. EPS file. The I/B/E/S Detail History guide (December 2009, appendix pp. 40–41) confirms that `6`–`9`, `N`–`Q`, and `Y` all denote quarterly horizons (quarters 1–4, 5–8, and more than eight quarters ahead). The inherited FPI population is correct; annual grouping by `year(FPEDATS)` remains a proxy because this source has no separate historical fiscal-year label.
- `ACTUAL` and `VALUE` are per-share EPS amounts, and the assigned adjusted Detail product is preserved. The surprise formula `(ACTUAL-VALUE)*100` yields cents per share without rounding source values. A 2009 guide example expresses EPS in currency units and the guide describes per-share corporate-action adjustment (pp. 10–14, 36–39). The source's scale check has median `ACTUAL` $0.15, median `VALUE` $0.16, and median surprise 0 cents; the 99th percentiles are $4.50, $5.00, and 106 cents, respectively. The extreme maxima are retained without clipping or winsorization; the assignment provides no alternative split basis or outlier rule.
- The guide defines `CURRFL` as the estimate's received Canadian-currency flag, while company-default-currency conversions are applied to Detail estimate values (pp. 15, 36–37). Thus `CURRFL='C'` does not imply `VALUE` is stored in CAD: among selected USD-report events, 571 have this received-currency flag. The actuals-file currency `CURR_ACT` is the relevant stored-actual descriptor. The selected-row currency table showed 190,053 USD report-currency events, of which 47 had CAD actuals; 962 selected events had CAD report currency and six had missing report currency. Requiring both `report_curr='USD'` and `CURR_ACT='USD'` leaves 190,006 common-USD events. The guide notes that actuals may remain in an earlier currency after a company-level currency change (pp. 17–18, 38–39); absent an assigned conversion source, dropping the 47 mismatches is the defensible unit-consistency repair.
- The guide says all Detail estimate values are adjusted to the company's basis, while estimate-level `PDF` records the primary/diluted basis on which the forecast was received (p. 18 and glossary pp. 52–53). The latest selected estimates show 74,175 `D` flags and 116,846 `P` flags. Because the values are already converted to company basis, filtering those flags would introduce an unsupported screen. `REVDATS` is the most recent date the estimate was confirmed accurate, not its forecast announcement date (glossary p. 52). It is present for all 190,006 final events; 166,329 review dates are after the forecast announcement and 106,004 are after the actual announce date, with a median 172 days after forecast announcement. These dates are diagnostic only and are not used for forecast selection; treating them as estimate dates would admit post-report information or discard eligible forecasts.
- Zero actual EPS is retained in the all-earnings median and excluded from profit/loss populations; profit and loss populations use positive and negative actuals. Zero/positive rates use the corresponding sign population; the small-positive profit rate is conditional on a positive surprise and uses `(0,3]` cents; the extreme-negative loss rate is conditional on a negative surprise and uses `≤−25` cents. These definitions match the paper text and Table 1 note (physical pp. 6, 10 / printed pp. 226, 230). The exact zero test and the `1e-10`-cent threshold tolerance are preserved; no economic rounding or near-zero smoothing is added.

## Date, vintage, ties, and population diagnostics

Among 2,073,916 candidate rows, 1,930,492 had a nonmissing actual announce-date field; 4,474 candidate forecast rows shared the actual's calendar date and 146,594 were later. The strict earlier-date screen retained 1,922,848 forecast rows covering 191,021 eligible `TICKER × FPEDATS × ANNDATS_ACT` event keys before currency screens. There were no eligible keys with multiple report dates or actual values; 132 keys did have multiple report-currency labels across their eligible forecast histories, so currency is applied after forecast selection. The latest-date/time audit found 8,109 event keys tied at their latest datetime (maximum tie size 11). The code resolves such ties deterministically by ascending analyst code, estimator code, and forecast value because the paper does not define a tie rule. It imposes no age cap.

The final common-USD sample has 190,006 quarterly events, 155,274 positive actuals, 33,347 negative actuals, and 1,385 zero actuals. It differs from the paper's 176,519 by +13,487 (+7.64%). No duplicate eligible ticker-period event keys were observed. The per-year paper and local counts and all 16 annual medians/frequencies are in [source_result_comparison.md](source_result_comparison.md). In particular, the final annual average is 11,875.375 quarterly events versus the paper's printed mean of 11,032; this does not change the regression sample size of 16.

Forecast age is `ANNDATS_ACT−ANNDATS`: median 36 days, 99th percentile 364 days, maximum 4,158 days; 1,854 selected observations exceed one year and three exceed five years. The maximum appears in the 1986 FPEDATS group. The delay is not explained by the currently available source descriptors. Since the paper prescribes the most timely forecast before report date and specifies no age cap, I retain the events and disclose the tail rather than impose a new screen.

Actual/report-date alignment is usually compact but not clean in every row. `ANNDATS_ACT−FPEDATS` has 1st percentile 9 days, median 27, 99th percentile 125, minimum −326, and maximum 4,040 days. There are 112 events with `ANNDATS_ACT < FPEDATS`, 112 more than one year after period end, and three more than five years after period end. The annual groups with the largest positive gaps are 1986 (4,040 days), 1997 (2,586), and 1993 (1,817). No report-period gap cutoff is applied because none is specified in the paper. The current source offers no revision/vintage dimension to determine whether these tails are delayed vendor activations, revised mappings, or other date issues.

The I/B/E/S guide says the Actuals-file date reflected before January 1999 was activation date and thereafter was announce date (p. 16). This current table provides both `ANNDATS_ACT` and `ACTDATS_ACT`; the build uses the explicitly labeled announce-date field. In the final sample, 187,680 rows have equal announcement and activation dates, 2,326 have announce date earlier than activation, none have announce date later, and none lack activation date. That pattern confirms the fields are not interchangeable but does not establish true announcement-date semantics for every archived row. The guide also says U.S. Detail History before June 1993 came from monthly archives and later history from production data (p. 6); the current historical vintage need not match Brown's contemporaneous files. `REVDATS` is a forecast confirmation date, not an original-file vintage stamp; the available source has no historical release/version snapshot. The separate Restated Actuals product is not assigned, and no author data are used.

## Annual and regression comparisons

The comparison artifact contains all 16 paper/local annual sample counts, medians, and rates, the source's time-series average row, and all nine trend fits. Each annual cell reproduces the paper's population definition but differs to varying degrees in values. Local annual sample counts exceed the source in most years, with one below-source year (1991); the source count difference cannot be attributed to duplicates. The 47 currency exclusions reduce local counts in 11 years. They remove 36 profit, seven loss, and four zero-actual events. They change the all-earnings median only in 1984 (−0.100 to −0.110 cents) and the profit median only in 1996 (0.530 to 0.510 cents); no loss median changes. Annual rate changes from this repair are small, with loss-rate changes only in 1994, 1996, and 1999.

All nine local slopes retain the source direction and are statistically different from zero under conventional two-sided OLS inference; Stata displays `p=0.001` for the all-earnings median and `p<0.001` for the other eight. The paper reports t-value magnitudes without a covariance description; the local negative extreme-loss coefficient has a signed t of −5.79 versus the source's printed magnitude 13.48, so significance is not the same numerical claim as a source-specified one-sided test. OLS with a constant and conventional standard errors is an explicit best-effort assumption, not paper-reported methodology.

## Remaining limitations

- `year(FPEDATS)` is a fiscal-year proxy, not an independently verified historical vendor label.
- The common-USD sample remains 13,487 events above the paper's count; population vintage, report-date convention, and historical screen differences remain possible but unproven explanations.
- Forecast-age and report-period-gap tails are observable but not explained by available source documentation. They are retained without invented caps.
- The current adjusted Detail values may have later split adjustments, precision, and historical revisions than the paper's source vintage.
- The paper does not identify its annual regression estimator or standard-error rule; conventional OLS is best effort.
