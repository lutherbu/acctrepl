# Review and repair findings

## Scope and execution

The retained baseline is the two-equation all-deals system in Equation (7) and Table 7 Panel A: `FE = α₀ + α₁ Growth` and `AR = β₁(FE − α₀* − α₁*Growth)`. The return equation has no free intercept. `Growth` in these equations is the offer-level mean I/B/E/S forecast; realized growth is used to construct `FE = realized growth − forecast growth`. The descriptive table is supporting evidence for the same one-offer sample, not an additional baseline model. The source definitions and tests are on physical pp. 12, 22, and 25–26.

The final run is `runtime/20261103T031654413973Z`; its SAS build, handoff, and both Stata cells succeeded. `runtime/latest.json` records the handoff and all eight table exports. The final SAS log has no `WARNING:`, `ERROR:`, or invalid `SUBSTR` argument. The handoff has 818 rows, 818 distinct `offer_id`s, no missing regression inputs, and exactly 60 observed monthly returns per row. Final tables are `tables/table7_joint_system.{md,csv,rtf,tex}` and `tables/table7_descriptive.{md,csv,rtf,tex}`. Validation reports two unblocked retained scopes.

## Source and construction checks

Table 1 (physical p. 13) reports 7,636 initial underwritten common-stock offerings from 1981–1990 and 1,179 final offerings from 1,006 firms. IPOs and seasoned offerings are both in scope; the first-offer-only condition appears only as a robustness check in footnote 22. The local SDC pool is 7,114 distinct U.S. public Common Shares candidates, including 3,582 IPOs and 3,532 seasoned offers. The local `BFO` underwriting field is missing for every candidate, so this is an approximate public-offer universe, not a verified underwritten sample. The local pool is 522 below the source initial count. The available `REGRIGHTS` field describes registration rights and is not a rights-offering flag; the field is mostly missing and the bounded local description scan found no literal rights category. I did not add unsupported SDC categories or filters to force the source count.

The build matches offers to dated `COMP.SEC_IDHIST` CUSIPs at the issue date and then to date-effective CCM/CRSP name records. It finds 4,866 unique GVKEY matches, 2,215 offers without a dated CUSIP match, and 33 ambiguous GVKEY mappings. It obtains 4,016 unique CRSP PERMNO links; 850 uniquely GVKEY-matched offers have no eligible primary or fallback Compustat link, and the final CRSP-link stage has no ambiguous PERMNOs. This is vintage-dependent coverage and cannot by itself explain the final difference from the paper.

Annual realized growth uses Compustat annual item 18 `IB`, as specified in footnote 13 (physical p. 29), rather than EPS or net income. The six log-earnings points follow the Table 7 note's offer year through offer year +5 (physical p. 26); the methods text instead says to start immediately before the offer (physical p. 14). The conflict is explicit in `design.md`, and the Table 7 note is the selected alignment. Negative and zero earnings are unavailable for the log fit, the endpoints are required, and missing interior years are allowed. The per-offer growth line includes an intercept and annual index; its slope is converted with `exp(slope)−1`. One consistent nonmissing currency is required across the earnings points. The selected Compustat history contains USD and CAD records; four offers with both endpoints present fail the currency-consistency/completeness condition. Two duplicate GVKEY/FYEAR keys remain after source filters; the build resolves them by latest FDATE, then PDATE, then DATADATE. Of 4,866 event-matched offers, 1,948 growth fits return an estimate.

I/B/E/S FPI 0/Measure EPS detail records in the issue-date horizon have no `CURR` and store LTG in percentage points, so the build divides their offer mean by 100. It averages every nonmissing detail record in the inclusive nine-month-before/three-month-after issue-date window, matching the paper's one-consensus-per-offer description (physical p. 22). It does not use a summary consensus for an arbitrarily selected month or impose a latest-per-analyst rule that the paper does not state. The build finds 12,820 detail rows and 1,921 offers with a valid forecast mean. Table 3 (physical p. 18) reports the −100% to 10%, 11% to 17%, and 18% to 100% ranges as portfolio descriptions; it does not prescribe a Table 7 exclusion screen. The corrected build retains all 48 detail rows outside those bins and reports them as a diagnostic. The unsupported `[−100%,100%]` screen in A1 did not change N, but it changed offer consensus means and is removed in A2.

The stock return is the compounded monthly CRSP return less the compounded value-weighted market return. To implement the three-month delay and five-year horizon (physical pp. 14, 26), the build uses month offsets +4 through +63, requiring all 60 stock and market observations. This gives 2,821 offers with complete returns. The final intersection is 818 offers with forecasts, a growth estimate, and complete returns. All 60 return periods are observed for every retained row. The observed source-to-final counts are therefore 7,114 local SDC candidates → 4,866 dated GVKEY matches → 4,016 CRSP links → 1,921 forecast-valid offers / 1,948 growth fits / 2,821 return-complete offers → 818 complete cases. Year-by-year counts are retained in the SAS listing.

The equal-offer final means are AR 0.122 (SD 1.592), realized growth 0.103 (SD 0.246), forecast growth 0.180 (SD 0.125), and FE −0.077 (SD 0.253). The offer-level mean identity is consistent: `0.0323 − 0.6072 × 0.1799 ≈ −0.0770`. This also resolves the initial label confusion: `Growth` is forecast growth, not realized growth. Table 2's means (physical p. 16: AR −0.127, realized growth 0.057, forecast growth 0.162, FE −0.106) are across 7,169 analyst forecasts, not equal-offer averages, and should not be treated as directly comparable to this sample.

## Estimator diagnosis and repair

A1 used native `nlsur, ifgnls` with a freely estimated cross-equation covariance. Its unrestricted fit converged after two FGNLS iterations on the 818-row handoff and returned α₀=.028456 (p=.090), α₁=−.568826 (p<.001), β₁=3.440095 (p<.001), α₀*=.028231 (p=.301), and α₁*=−.772727 (p<.001), with reported LL −1,408.917. Its efficiency LR was χ²(2)=6.408 (p=.0406). Its naive restricted fit ran through 117 FGNLS iterations, ending with parameter change 9.61e−6 and covariance change 3.33e−6; the reported naive LR was χ²(2)=.129 (p=.9375), despite the native joint star-parameter Wald test χ²(2)=88.057 (p<.001). This is a structural identification problem, not a convergence failure alone: FE is both the first-equation outcome and a return-equation regressor. With an unrestricted cross-equation covariance, the reduced-form return means do not separately identify β₁ and the two star parameters. Under that covariance, the naive restrictions have only one testable reduced-form mean restriction, so the reported two-df naive LR and coefficient Wald tests are not valid structural inference. The same argument does not automatically invalidate the efficiency restriction, which sets both reduced-form return mean coefficients to zero; A1's efficiency LR is retained as a diagnostic, not rejected on this identification argument.

Because the paper states nonlinear weighted least squares but does not give its weight matrix or residual covariance, A2 does not claim to recover the author's weighting. It uses a direct native `mlexp` conditional Gaussian likelihood: a normal density for FE given forecast growth, multiplied by a normal density for AR given observed FE and forecast growth. The conditional equation errors are assumed independent with separate constant variances. This diagonal covariance restriction identifies the recursive system and makes the source's two-constraint restrictions testable as nested likelihoods. It is an explicit best-effort assumption. The unrestricted fit converged in six iterations, the efficiency fit in five, and the naive fit in six. The estimated standard deviations are positive by construction (`exp(lnsfe)≈.241`, `exp(lnsar)≈1.351`); all seven parameters, including both log variances, have finite standard errors. At the fitted nonzero β₁, the nonlinear return parameters map one-to-one to its three OLS coefficients.

The native OLS checks in the Stata log verify labels and parameter mapping. Regressing FE on forecast growth returns the unrestricted `a0` and `a1` exactly. `nlcom` gives the return-equation mapping `−β₁α₀*=−.063789`, `−β₁α₁*=2.521239`, and `β₁=3.480003`; direct OLS of AR on FE and forecast growth gives the same coefficients. Source Table 7 Panel B has .063, 1.163, and −.161, which also equal the algebraic transforms of its Panel A values to rounding. The local mapping is sound, while local estimates differ substantially from the source.

## Remaining limits

The main limits are the unspecified source WLS weights/covariance, local SDC underwriting and rights-offering coverage, the 7,114 versus 7,636 initial-offer gap, vintage-dependent identifier/return coverage, and the conflicting realized-growth start date. The conditional independence and homoskedasticity assumptions are not reported by the paper. They are disclosed for identification and inference, not represented as an exact recovered author estimator. The 818-row fit is 361 observations below source Table 7 N=1,179; the available evidence does not attribute this difference to one single screen.
