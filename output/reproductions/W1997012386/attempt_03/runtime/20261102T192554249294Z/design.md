# Design: Ahmed et al. (2002), Table 3, 1993–1998 levels

## Frozen scope and source evidence

This attempt covers the two frozen scopes: Table 3 Panel A (CONMKT, levels, 1993–1998; expected N=704) and Panel B (CONACC, levels, 1993–1998; expected N=632). Their full specification is Equation (2): STDROA, DIV, LEV, ROA, SIZE, SALESGRO, and RNDADV, with an intercept (original PDF physical pp. 10, 17–18). The paper says firm-specific six-year means are used for levels; Table 3 reports White-corrected p-values, one-tailed where the sign is predicted (physical p. 18). The baseline estimator is not named, so the direct cross-sectional OLS estimate with an intercept and heteroskedasticity-robust covariance is a best-effort translation. Stata's reported p-values are two-sided.

The supporting Equation (1) fit is also required to construct CONMKT. The paper estimates firm and year fixed effects on 1993–1998 BTM observations with current and six lagged annual total returns, then defines CONMKT as minus the firm effect (physical pp. 6–7). Its initial support population is 730 rated firms, before the additional screens yielding the two Table 3 sample sizes (physical p. 12). The article gives no exact BTM components, return-compounding convention, accounting missing-item convention, Compustat row filters, or link selection rules. The choices below are explicit best-effort choices and are not tuned to published estimates.

## Sources and field mapping

The annual accounting source is catalog-listed `COMP.FUNDA` (`/storage/sas/base/comp/d_na/funda.sas7bdat`). The selected fields and catalog labels are: `GVKEY` (Global Company Key), `DATADATE` (Data Date), `FYEAR` (Data Year - Fiscal), `INDFMT`, `CONSOL`, `POPSRC`, `DATAFMT`, `CURCD` (ISO Currency Code), `AT` (Assets - Total), `CEQ` (Common/Ordinary Equity - Total), `IB` (Income Before Extraordinary Items), `DVC` (Dividends Common/Ordinary), `DLTT` (Long-Term Debt - Total), `SALE` (Sales/Turnover (Net)), `XRD` (Research and Development Expense), `XAD` (Advertising Expense), `XDP` (Depreciation Expense (Schedule VI)), and `OANCF` (Operating Activities - Net Cash Flow). The current Compustat filters are `INDFMT='INDL'`, `CONSOL='C'`, `POPSRC='D'`, and `DATAFMT='STD'`. The Xpressfeed data guide gives these as its standard company annual extraction keys and defines `D` as domestic U.S./Canadian population (2011 guide, physical pp. 9–11). The paper's “1998 Annual Industrial Compustat (Primary, Secondary, and Tertiary) files” does not state the exact current-format filter codes; the filters are a documented contemporary proxy, not proof of the authors' extraction settings.

Legacy S&P Research Insight item maps were checked against the cached provider-authored 2004 data guide: `AT` A6 (physical p. 136); `DLTT` A9 (p. 163); `SALE` A12 (p. 270); `IB` A18 (p. 250); `DP` A14 (p. 242); `DVC` A21 (p. 292); `XAD` A45 (p. 292); `XRD` A46 (p. 310); `CEQ` A60 (p. 142); `XDP` A103 (p. 707); and `OANCF` A308 (p. 871). The guide separately maps A123 to `IBC`, “Income Before Extraordinary Items (Cash Flow)” (p. 850), so `IB` (A18) is used for the paper's income-statement measure. The paper calls the CONACC component “depreciation expense” but does not name a Compustat item. The 2004 guide maps `XDP` to depreciation expense from Schedule VI (A103), while `DP` (A14) is depreciation and amortization; the guide says XDP stopped being collected on January 21, 1997 (physical p. 707). The 2011 Xpressfeed guide includes current `DP` but does not list `XDP`; accordingly the build uses current `DP` as a broad proxy, explicitly not an exact item match. It also computes `XDP`-based coverage counts for comparison. No missing depreciation, income, cash-flow, or other monetary item is set to zero.

The S&P rating screen uses catalog-listed `COMP.ADSPRATE.SPLTICRM` (field label “S&P Domestic Long Term Issuer Credit Rating”), requiring a nonmissing record between 1993-01-01 and 1998-08-31. The provider's 2004 guide says its “S&P Domestic Long-Term Issuer Credit Rating - Current” field represented the issuer's senior debt rating before 1998-09-01 and an issuer credit rating after that date (physical p. 53). The local mnemonic differs from the guide's legacy mnemonic (`SPDRC`), so the field crosswalk to the local `SPLTICRM` is best-effort; the post-change issuer-rating months are excluded, and rating strings are not numerically recoded. The paper only requires a rating to be available during the six-year period for the support cohort (physical p. 12).

CRSP inputs are catalog-listed `CRSP.MSF`, `CRSP.MSI`, `CRSP.DSENAMES`, and `CRSP.CCMXPF_LINKTABLE`. The provider's 2012 CRSP stock guide defines first-digit share type 1 as ordinary common shares; ADRs are type 3 and are excluded (physical p. 115, printed p. 111). `MSF.PERMCO` is retained to construct company market equity from all active ordinary-common issues in the same company and month. The CCM guide defines `LC` as a completed standard link, `LU` as an unresearched CUSIP link, `LS` as a link valid for its security only, `P`/`C` as primary markers, `J`/`N` as secondary markers, and `USEDFLAG=1` as a used link (physical pp. 8–9, 21, 23; printed pp. 4–5, 17, 19). The guide says other securities in the same `PERMCO` may link to other GVKEYs under `LS`; consequently primary-link uniqueness does not establish a unique company mapping. The main primary security still uses `LINKTYPE in ('LC','LU')`, `LINKPRIM in ('P','C')`, `USEDFLAG=1`, and inclusive effective dates; a month with multiple eligible primary PERMNOs for a GVKEY is unavailable rather than resolved arbitrarily. LC-only counts remain an audit comparison. The company-value audit separately scans active ordinary-common shares sharing each rated support PERMCO/month and all used `LC`/`LU`/`LS` link rows, including secondary markers. It sums price times shares across those shares only when every class has a valid market value and their active links identify exactly one GVKEY matching the Compustat firm. An ordinary-common issue with no active link is counted separately; because it has no CCM link row, it is not assigned the `J`/`N` secondary marker. It can be included when it introduces no competing GVKEY; a PERMCO/month linked to multiple GVKEYs or missing any class market value has no company BTM for that firm-year. The primary-security market value is retained for comparison only. This rule does not screen all multi-class firms.

Currency translation is applied before cross-year or cross-firm monetary comparisons. `CURCD` identifies the reporting currency for each annual period (Xpressfeed 2011, physical p. 86). The guide identifies annual income-statement totals and balance-sheet totals in millions (physical pp. 19–24), so converted Compustat monetary amounts remain USD millions; `SIZE` is the log of USD-million assets. The cached Xpressfeed guide says to use daily exchange rates for balance-sheet and price data and the 12-month average rate for income-statement and cash-flow data (physical p. 86). For each currency, daily rates are taken from `COMP.G_EXRT_DLY` (`FROMCURD`, `TOCURD`, `DATADATE`, `EXRATD`) and flow rates from `COMP.G_EXRT_MTH` (`FROMCURM`, `TOCURM`, `DATADATE`, `EXRAT12M`). The provider's cross-rate formula is reported value × (1 / GBP-to-reporting rate) × GBP-to-USD rate (physical p. 87). A rate is used only when the relevant currency-date key has one unique rate; missing or ambiguous rates leave converted amounts missing. USD values use factor 1. `CURRTR` is not used as a generic multiplier. No country filter is imposed. FY1998 reporting dates in the selected local Compustat population extend through 1999-05-31, so both FX sources retain exact-date rows through that maximum fiscal date. The predecessor's 1998-12-31 limit is separately audited; a source descriptor from another date is never transferred. For same-date `DLTT/AT`, the balance-sheet conversion factor cancels; for same-date `(XRD+XAD)/SALE`, the 12-month flow factor cancels. Those ratios use the observed source amounts directly and do not require an otherwise missing FX rate. The build retains their converted versions as diagnostics; no source values are filled.

## Construction windows and order

1. Read the exact catalog-listed source tables above with early date and field restrictions. Annual Compustat rows cover FY1986–1998 for fiscal return boundaries and FY1992–1998 for the Table 3 variables; FY1992 sales is the base for FY1993 sales growth. Fiscal labels and `DATADATE` are retained separately. The full local FY1998 industrial population reaches 1999-05-31, so CRSP MSF, DSENAMES/CCM effective-history candidates, and FX source rows extend through that observed end date. This raw support period follows actual report dates; it does not alter the Jan. 1993–Dec. 1998 calendar market-model sample or the rating screen ending 1998-08-31. The build reports exact source-date and return-month coverage against the predecessor's calendar-1998 cap. Duplicate Compustat firm-date keys are reported and excluded from derived joins rather than hidden with `NODUPKEY`.
2. For each 1993–1998 firm-year, construct BTM as converted `CEQ` divided by CRSP company market equity. For each fiscal date, use the latest eligible primary-link month-end on or before `DATADATE` and no more than one month old; use that month and its `PERMCO` to identify every active ordinary-common CRSP issue. Each class value is absolute monthly `PRC` × `SHROUT` / 1,000 (USD millions), and company market equity is their sum. A company BTM is available only if each included class has a valid price/share value and active used `LC`/`LU`/`LS` links across the common classes map to exactly the same single GVKEY as the Compustat firm. Unmapped common issues are counted and may be included with the PERMCO group when no other GVKEY link conflicts. If mapping is conflicting or any eligible class lacks its market value, BTM is unavailable for that firm-year; the primary value is diagnostic only. This applies a targeted unresolved-company mapping rule and keeps coherent single- and multi-class observations. The paper does not identify the preferred book-equity adjustment or CRSP price source. Negative book equity is retained when the company market ratio is defined. The build records actual fiscal dates, selected price dates, the gap in days, and 1999 price dates.
3. Compound CRSP monthly total returns (`RET`, with dividends) within each fiscal-year interval defined by adjacent Compustat `DATADATE` values. A fiscal return is available only with 12 nonmissing monthly returns and a unique eligible GVKEY–PERMNO link for each month. Fiscal returns for 1987–1998 supply `RET_t` through `RET_{t-6}` for the 1993–1998 BTM rows. The diagnostic tabulates every annual interval, observed monthly row count, valid return count, and whether a complete interval would have been lost under the predecessor's 1998-12-31 source cap. This fiscal alignment follows the paper's definition of `t` as fiscal year-end (physical p. 6), although its annual compounding convention is unreported.
4. Estimate the support fixed-effects model on firms with six 1993–1998 BTM observations and all seven returns for each observation, plus a nonmissing senior-debt-rating screen record. The auxiliary fit is a PROC GLM model with GVKEY and fiscal-year fixed effects and seven annual return regressors. Center the fitted GVKEY least-squares means across support firms, then set CONMKT to minus that relative firm effect. Balanced six-year histories are required; this centers firm effects at zero as described in the Table 1 footnote (physical p. 14).
5. For FY1993–1998, construct annual `ROA=IB_USD/AT_USD`, `DIV=DVC_USD/AT_USD`, `LEV=DLTT/AT`, `SIZE=ln(AT_USD)`, `SALESGRO=SALE_USD/SALE_USD_{t-1}-1`, and `RNDADV=(XRD+XAD)/SALE`. LEV and RNDADV are formed from same-date amounts in the same reporting currency; their applicable conversion factor cancels exactly. Their diagnostics compare the native-currency ratios with ratios computed after USD conversion where rates are available. `STDROA` is the sample standard deviation of available annual ROA observations, requiring at least two valid years. Each other firm-level predictor is the mean over its available valid years, requiring at least one; the build retains `n_roa` and each predictor's valid-year count for transparency. The paper says that the explanatory variables are firm-specific six-year means, but its sample description distinguishes the explicit six-year BTM and return-history requirements from those later accounting means and does not state a minimum observed-year count for the other variables (physical pp. 10, 12). The available-year means are therefore a best-effort reading of the source. Missing inputs remain missing, and zero denominators are not imputed.
6. For the market-data screen described with the sample, estimate a standard market model of monthly CRSP `RET` on CRSP `MSI.VWRETD` for January 1993–December 1998, separately by GVKEY. `VWRETD` is the value-weighted market return including dividends per the catalog label. Require at least 24 nonmissing stock/market return pairs; retain the PROC REG beta and mean squared residual as auxiliary variables. The paper does not state whether these monthly windows are calendar or fiscal aligned; calendar months are used for this six-calendar-year screen.
7. The CONMKT fit sample requires the six-year support estimate, available-year predictors under the rule above, and the 24-month market-model screen. The CONACC fit adds an available-year mean of annual `(IB_USD + DP_USD - OANCF_USD)/AT_USD`, multiplied by −1, requiring at least one valid annual ratio. DP is a broader depreciation-and-amortization proxy, not an exact match for the paper's depreciation-expense wording; the XDP-based alternative is used only for an availability diagnostic. Expected final Ns are 704 and 632; observed counts will be compared after diagnostics. Table 3 excludes industry sensitivity controls from the selected baseline column.

## Estimation and inference

Each frozen Table 3 fit is a direct Stata 18 OLS regression with an intercept and the seven Equation (2) predictors. `vce(robust)` provides heteroskedasticity-robust covariance; no clustering, fixed effects, or weights are reported for these cross-sections. The published table's predicted-sign p-values are one-sided where signed hypotheses are reported, whereas the template export uses Stata's two-sided p-values. Coefficients and inference will be compared without changing sample screens or estimation to match the paper. A descriptive table will summarize each actual analysis sample and report variable-specific counts so CONACC's smaller available sample remains visible.

### Company market equity and support sample

The paper defines BTM as firm book-to-market at fiscal year-end and describes the underlying construct as book equity relative to market value of equity (Equation (1), physical pp. 6–7; sample discussion, p. 12). Consolidated `CEQ` is therefore paired with company common market equity. The CRSP unit check confirms that price is in dollars and shares outstanding are in thousands; `abs(PRC) × SHROUT / 1,000` is USD millions, consistent with the Compustat equity units. The bounded audit has 55,862 support GVKEY–PERMCO–month rows for the 726 firms passing the primary-link/rating screen. It finds 1,444 of these firm-month rows with multiple ordinary-common PERMNOs; all have complete class prices and shares. In these multiclass rows the company total differs from primary-security value by as much as $68,940.08 million and reaches 18.38812 times the primary value. These differences demonstrate why primary-link uniqueness is not a test of company-wide equity.

At the monthly audit grain, 182 support GVKEY–PERMCO–month rows have active common-class links to more than one GVKEY, while 17 rows contain an ordinary-common PERMNO with no active link; all 17 also have multiple common issues. These are separate outcomes: an unmapped share is not itself a second-GVKEY conflict. At the BTM fiscal dates, four distinct firms have a conflicting GVKEY mapping, affecting 15 firm-year BTM rows across 1993–1998 (2, 2, 2, 2, 3, and 4 by year). One additional firm has an unmapped common class in FY1996 and FY1997, but only one GVKEY is linked, so the class is included in company equity; the other 15 conflicting firm-years remain unavailable. Thus 722 of the 726 rated firms have six company-BTM years. This is a targeted unresolved-mapping condition, not a general multi-class exclusion: 98 coherent multi-class BTM firm-years remain in the balanced support panel.

The full return/BTM screen yields 1,932 balanced firms (11,592 firm-years) before the rating screen. The corrected primary-link rating screen yields 726 firms. Requiring mapped company BTM for all six years leaves 722 firms (4,332 observations); their `CONMKT` has a centered support mean of 4.83e−16. Of those 722, 140 have observed `RNDADV`, 140 pass the market-model screen, 660 have DP-based `CONACC`, and 398 have XDP-based coverage. The final CONMKT and CONACC samples are both 140 firms; 103 of those have XDP-based CONACC coverage. The predecessor's final A2 sample was 105. A bounded comparison using extended-horizon primary-security BTM had 141 final firms; applying coherent company market equity removes one firm (GVKEY 005073). This isolates the effect of the company-scope repair from that interim primary-only result.

Relative to accepted A2, the corrected A3 has 722 rather than 636 rated balanced support firms, and 140 rather than 105 final firms. The paper reports 730 rated support firms, 704 Panel A firms, and 632 Panel B firms (physical p. 12; Table 3, physical pp. 17–18). These are observed discrepancies, not sample targets. The extended fiscal horizon demonstrably restores required late BTM prices, return months, and exact-date FX; company equity further removes one otherwise eligible firm. The joint count change cannot be assigned solely to one repair because the BTM denominator and support cohort also changed.

## Known unresolved assumptions

The local Compustat and CRSP vintages do not establish the authors' exact 1998 source vintage or CCM selection policy. The paper does not specify CEQ adjustments, annual-return compounding, Compustat row filters, treatment of missing items, or minimum available-year counts for its accounting means. The rating-field crosswalk from local `SPLTICRM` to the legacy field remains best-effort. The company market-equity construction uses CRSP's active ordinary-common classes and PERMCO grouping with used CCM `LC`/`LU`/`LS` links; the locally observed ambiguous mappings are left unavailable, but historical coverage may differ from the paper's CRSP database. The paper calls for depreciation expense without naming an item; current DP remains a broader proxy and XDP availability is diagnostic. The pronounced RNDADV and MSE descriptive differences remain unresolved. None of these uncertainties justifies replacing observed missing values or imposing an unstated sample filter.
