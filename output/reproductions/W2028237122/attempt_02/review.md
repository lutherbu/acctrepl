# Independent review: W2028237122, attempt 02

## Scope and source checks

I reviewed the assigned Figure 1 temporal-trend fits for avoiding quarterly losses, earnings decreases, and negative earnings surprises. The paper defines standardized differences from adjacent earnings bins and fits each series on year (paper pp. 428–430; Figure 1, p. 430). I inspected physical PDF pages 5–8 to resolve the formulas and labels, and page 15 (printed p. 437, “Split-Adjusted Data”) to verify the input share basis. Footnote 12 uses a **plus** between the binomial term and the adjacent-bin variance term. Footnote 15 standardizes annual bins to N=1,000; footnote 13 assigns zero to the right-of-zero bin. The build preserves 1984 lag history, forms 1985–2002 annual observations, and retains the full three-day announcement-return window, including event dates in 2003.

The paper’s main analysis is split-adjusted. Its unadjusting discussion describes a separate I/B/E/S-factor sensitivity analysis. The frozen catalog includes adjusted `IBES.ACT_EPSUS` and `IBES.DET_EPSUS`, but not historical I/B/E/S stock prices or the documented Detail History Adjustments file described in the 2009 guide (physical p. 16). The available `SPLIT_GUIDANCE` file is not documented as that adjustments file, and does not establish a cumulative share-factor terminal basis. The adjusted earnings therefore cannot be paired defensibly with the available CRSP price. The executable build instead uses `ACTU_EPSUS`/`DETU_EPSUS` and relative CRSP `CFACSHR` ratios dated to the values and quarter-opening price. This is internally aligned to the CRSP share basis and resembles the dated-factor logic in the WRDS 2006 unadjusted-data note (pp. 5–6), but extending its factor application to the quarter-opening price is an explicit approximation. It removes the rounding behavior described by the paper. I classify it as an **unadjusted, sensitivity-style approximation**; it is neither the paper’s split-adjusted primary nor its exact I/B/E/S-factor sensitivity.

I checked the local catalog (`/storage/sas/data_description.txt` and `output/feasibility_runs/resources/catalog.json`) for field availability and types. Actual values are screened with `CURR_ACT='USD'`; forecasts with `REPORT_CURR='USD'`. The 2009 Detail History guide (physical pp. 18–19) says estimates are converted to company/report currency and `CURRFL` records received currency. The audit confirms that `CURR` is blank in this local U.S. product and that blank or `C` `CURRFL` can occur with `REPORT_CURR='USD'`; screening on received-currency provenance would therefore select the wrong stored-value basis. Forecast FPI codes include quarterly horizons 1–12 and Y, consistent with the 2009 guide (physical p. 41) and current WRDS IBES guide. The build keeps zero EPS values and does not impose an unsupported forecast lookback cap.

The paper does not give a field/date rule for its beginning-quarter I/B/E/S price. The code uses the first nonzero CRSP close from quarter start through ten calendar days later and reports the selected age without a separate age screen. For a quarter ending 31 July, the implemented opening date is 1 May. The current actual’s `ANNDATS` is the event-date proxy; the 2009 guide warns that pre-1999 actual announcement dates can reflect activation (physical p. 16). A unique effective-dated historical CRSP `NCUSIP`/PERMNO link is required. Nontrading announcement dates map to the next CRSP market date, and CAR is summed over three trading days against `VWRETD`; quantile convention and covariance estimator remain unspecified by the paper. Conventional OLS standard errors are used, and reported table p-values are two-sided.

## Repairs and construction audit

I rebuilt the SAS construction in attempt 02 and added explicit t−4 date-gap and price-age diagnostics. I found and repaired a link-audit error inherited from the earlier implementation: the old diagnostic counted missing name-history rows after a grouped left join, where the grouping emitted one row even when no history matched. The repaired audit counts `nperm=0` as no PERMNO match and separately counts unique matches without SIC. This changes diagnostic reporting only; it does not change link eligibility, filters, the declared sample, or fitted data. I did not add an age restriction or tune any threshold, date rule, or sample count to the paper.

The completed SAS run (`runtime/20261102T195431023789Z/build.lst`, pp. 5–7, 14–23) reports:

- 333,156 current actuals; 284,934 unique t−4 matches, of which 283,757 have an exact `PENDS` match and 1,177 are one calendar day away. There are 48,222 without a unique lag match.
- 321,290 unique effective-dated PERMNO/SIC links, 11,866 no-PERMNO matches, zero missing-SIC among unique matches, and zero ambiguous effective links.
- 229,688 timely forecast matches, 318,099 price matches, and 320,001 complete three-day CARs. After all threshold inputs, SIC exclusions, and CAR completeness, 163,938 remain before trimming; 1,640 low and 1,639 high CAR observations are removed, leaving 160,659 with no duplicate PERMNO/PENDS keys.
- The final sample is 49,174 (44.1%) larger than the paper’s 111,485 firm-quarters. Current diagnostics do not isolate one cause. I do not attribute the difference solely to data vintage; the unavailable I/B/E/S price basis, return implementation, linking, and actual-date coverage remain possible sources.

## Baseline fit comparison

Each assigned regression has 18 annual observations. Slopes are standardized-difference units per calendar year. The paper reports slopes, t-statistics, and adjusted R² in Figure 1 (p. 430); the executed table supplies conventional OLS SEs and two-sided p-values.

| Trend | N | Slope (SE) | t | Two-sided p | Adjusted R² | Paper slope / t / adjusted R² |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Avoid losses | 18 | −0.0344 (0.0304) | −1.13 | 0.274 | 0.016 | −0.0361 / −1.63 / 0.09 |
| Avoid earnings decreases | 18 | −0.0960 (0.0515) | −1.86 | 0.081 | 0.127 | −0.1024 / −1.62 / 0.09 |
| Avoid negative surprises | 18 | −0.9758 (0.0721) | −13.54 | <0.001 | 0.915 | −1.218 / −13.91 / 0.92 |

The decline slope for negative surprises is strongly negative and significant; the loss slope is not significant by a two-sided 10% test, and the decrease slope has two-sided p=0.081. Directional one-sided probabilities would differ, but do not change the point estimates. The supporting pooled Chow restriction rejects equality of the decrease and surprise slopes relative to the loss slope, F(2,48)=94.86, p<0.001, matching the paper’s stated 1% conclusion. The fitted slopes and annual outcomes differ from the paper, alongside the larger construction sample; no sample or transformation was changed to improve the fit.
