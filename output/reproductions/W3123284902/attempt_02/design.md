# Design: W3123284902 Table 5 Model 4

## Frozen scope

The assigned scopes are Table 5 Model 4 Panels A, B, and C only. Each panel is a monthly Fama–MacBeth regression from July 1966 through December 2002 (438 calendar months). Panel A uses the most recently available fiscal-year NOA and Accruals measures, Panel B uses the preceding fiscal-year measures, and Panel C uses the measures from two preceding fiscal years; the accounting measures themselves are each scaled by prior-year assets. Size and book-to-market remain at the latest annual refresh in all three panels. I hold an annual record for 12 monthly observations beginning four calendar months after its fiscal year-end, then refresh it when the next annual record becomes eligible. The paper gives the four-month minimum and annual refresh frequency but not the precise month-assignment algorithm, so this is an explicit best-effort timing convention.

The paper’s broad sample statement is 1,625,570 firm-months from July 1964 through December 2002 and 141,254 firm-years from fiscal 1963 through 2000 (physical pp. 10, 12–13; printed pp. 306, 308–309). Those counts are comparison outcomes, not sample targets. The Table 5 return regressions require return history back to July 1963 for the first July 1966 observation’s 36-month lag window; I retain that earlier return history for construction. The article does not report the firm-month N in any Table 5 cross-section.

The Table 1 note’s fiscal-year count does not explicitly cap Table 5’s annual predictors. The fiscal 1965–2000 window on physical p. 30 (printed p. 326) is stated for the separate Mishkin test. I therefore apply Table 5’s four-month availability gap and 12-month refresh rule to all local annual records, using each statement only from its eligible month and ending the prior snapshot at the next eligible refresh. This allows fiscal 2001 and, for firms whose fiscal 2002 reports clear the gap, fiscal 2002 records into the 2001–2002 return tests. It avoids extending fiscal 2000 values beyond the stated annual refresh interval or using later reports before their eligibility month. The paper does not give publication dates or explicitly reconcile Table 1’s year range with Table 5’s end date, so this is a documented best-effort reading of the monthly specification.

## Sources and identifiers

Read only the catalog-listed `COMP.FUNDA`, `COMP.SEC_MTH`, `COMP.SEC_MTHPRC`, `COMP.G_EXRT_DLY`, `CRSP.MSF`, `CRSP.MSENAMES`, `CRSP.MSEDELIST`, and `CRSP.CCMXPF_LINKTABLE` tables. Annual accounting fields come from standard, consolidated, domestic Compustat annual records (`DATAFMT='STD'`, `CONSOL='C'`, `POPSRC='D'`). I prefer the universal `INDL` row for each GVKEY/FYEAR and use an `FS` row only if that key has no INDL record; SAS audits overlap and required-field completeness by format. The paper expressly includes the financial industry (physical p. 16, printed p. 312, footnote 16), so no SIC-based financial exclusion is applied. This follows the provider's description of INDL as the core annual format and FS as supplemental (S&P/WRDS 2026 provider slide 16; see the campaign resource documentation pointer).

For monthly securities, I keep CRSP common-share codes 10/11 on NYSE, AMEX, or Nasdaq exchange codes 1/2/3, using effective-dated `MSENAMES` records. I link returns to GVKEY through dated CCM links of types LC/LU and primary markers P/C; open link boundaries are retained. Ambiguous GVKEY-month primary-link matches will be counted and left out of the firm-month handoff rather than resolved by arbitrary PERMNO ordering. When contemporaneous candidate links remain, dated CCM `LIID` agreement with annual `HIID` is a tie-break after CCM primary-link priority. For annual price-currency evidence, I match Compustat `FUNDA.HIID` exactly to `SEC_MTH` and `SEC_MTHPRC` on GVKEY, issue, and fiscal-end month, and accept only a unique dated price whose security-month marker is `PRIMISS='P'`. I audit all company-month primary issues and price agreements separately, but do not take a currency from another issue if HIID is absent, ambiguous, or not marked primary. Company shares are taken from Compustat `CSHO`, not inferred by summing issue shares: the provider defines annual CSHO as millions of all common shares outstanding (Research Insight 2004, CSHO/A25, printed p. 602), while the paper explicitly forms market value as fiscal-year closing price times company shares (physical p. 13, printed p. 309). This preserves the paper’s primary-price/company-share construction.

## Variables and timing

The monthly outcome is CRSP `RET`, the raw total stock return including distributions. I combine an observed same-month delisting return as `(1+RET)*(1+DLRET)-1`; when `RET` is missing but a valid delisting return exists, the delisting return is retained. Other missing returns stay missing. Return windows are constructed on each PERMNO’s calendar-month sequence and require a nonmissing return for every expected month: `Ret(-1:-1)` is t−1, `Ret(-12:-2)` compounds 11 months, and Table 5’s `Ret(-36:-13)` compounds 24 months through t−13. The body text on physical p. 13 (printed p. 309) says the last window ends at t−12; the Table 5 note on physical p. 24 (printed p. 320) says t−13. The table-specific definition controls.

Annual NOA uses the source equation on physical pp. 10–12 (printed pp. 306–308):

`NOA = (DLC + DLTT + MIB + PSTK + CEQ − CHE) / prior-year AT`.

The two AT terms in operating assets and liabilities cancel algebraically, so current AT is not required for the numerator; prior-year AT remains required as the denominator. The provider legacy item index confirms Compustat #38 is `MIB`, #130 is `PSTK`, and #60 is `CEQ`. Annual Accruals use:

`Accruals = (ΔACT − ΔCHE − ΔLCT + ΔDLC + ΔTXP − DP) / prior-year AT`.

Both measures use actual GVKEY/fiscal-year joins and require the prior fiscal-year record; a missing or nonconsecutive prior period is not filled from an adjacent row. Missing `DLC`, `DLTT`, `MIB`, `PSTK`, and `TXP` are imputed to zero only where used in the paper’s stated constructions (physical p. 11, printed p. 307). Other missing fields, including `CHE`, `ACT`, `LCT`, `DP`, `CEQ`, and denominator `AT`, remain missing. Current and prior accounting amounts must share a currency code for annual changes and scaling.

Annual market value is `PRCC_F * CSHO` as stated in Table 1’s notes (physical pp. 12–13, printed pp. 308–309). The handoff audits Compustat’s historical primary issue (`HIID`), dated security-month primary marker, and `CURCDM` price currency against `CURCD` for the accounting record. Book equity and market value are translated to USD using the provider’s GBP cross-rate formula and the latest paired daily FX quote on or before fiscal year-end, allowing a maximum seven-calendar-day lookback. Both exchange-rate legs must be positive and observed on the same quote date. Actual/carried-forward provenance is retained per leg without imposing an unsupported equal-type requirement. FX coverage gaps leave the USD market value or book value unavailable; they are not filled with zero or a presumed exchange rate. Size is `ln(MV in USD millions)` and book-to-market is `ln(CEQ_USD/MV_USD)`. Negative book values are excluded as the paper states (physical p. 22, printed p. 318); zero book value and nonpositive market value also cannot yield the source-defined logarithms.

## Estimation and output

Each monthly cross-section is an unweighted OLS regression with an intercept and the five asset-pricing controls plus the panel-specific Accruals and NOA. The paper does not specify the estimator for individual monthly cross-sections; OLS is the best-effort choice. The `xtfmb` package computes the time-series average and conventional Fama–MacBeth standard errors with its default full coefficient covariance across monthly fits. No fixed effects, weights, or HAC lag option are reported, so none are added. Monthly complete-case N and full-rank counts are audited separately in SAS; 438 estimable monthly fits are the target evidence, not a 438-firm observation count. Stata tables follow the assigned template, which prints two-sided p-values; comparison to the paper uses the printed time-series t-statistics.

No trimming or winsorization is used. Footnote 23 explicitly says that tests based on portfolios and Fama–MacBeth cross-sectional regressions are not trimmed (physical p. 30, printed p. 326). No Table 4 characteristic-matched portfolios, later Mishkin system, or later robustness screens are in scope.
