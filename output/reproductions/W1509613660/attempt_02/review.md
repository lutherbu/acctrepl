## Source and code review

The target is the pooled 80% threshold column of Table 4 (paper physical p. 9). It has a common intercept and three forecast-revision-by-ability slopes; it does not include a standalone revision slope or ability-group intercepts. The paper reports N=15,446, 918 firms, and 1,410 analysts. Its slopes are 0.2355, 0.0890, and 0.0154, with t-statistics 3.957, 3.152, and 0.225, and adjusted R-squared 0.0014. Published pairwise slope tests are F/p = 5.040/0.025, 5.978/0.015, and 1.012/0.315.

The sample implementation follows the paper's physical pp. 3–5 and 14: one-year-ahead annual EPS revisions in 1990–1994, seven-year support from 1988–1994, at least five estimator codes (broker houses) in each support year, and at least five scoreable forecasts for an analyst-firm pair in the preceding two calendar years with at least one in each year. Forecast usefulness requires strictly smaller absolute error than the same-period consensus error; ties are not useful. Superior is at least 80% and inferior at most 20%, following the stated prose cutoffs. Fiscal-year-end changes are excluded; the paper's December-only screen belongs to its later spillover analysis. Winsorization retains observations and applies the 2nd/98th percentile cutoffs to return and revision before forming interactions.

The construction uses unadjusted I/B/E/S Detail forecasts, I/B/E/S Summary History, Compustat annual EPSPX, dated security history, Xpressfeed FX rates, and CRSP daily/security files. Forecast events use ACTDATS, consistent with the paper's “entered” date definition (footnote 1, physical p. 14). Detail VALUE's per-share basis is ANNDATS, with ACTDATS used only when ANNDATS is missing. Summary MEDEST is based at STATPERS. Compustat annual EPSPX is based at fiscal DATADATE; RDQ is used only as the actual's release-availability date. Historical actuals are Compustat EPSPX, not I/B/E/S actuals. Of 3,463,076 Detail EPS rows, 3,358,283 have a unique effective ticker/PERMNO link at ACTDATS, 872 have multiple PERMNO matches and are excluded, and 103,921 have no effective link. Summary History has 546,875 unique keys; the standardized annual Compustat extract has 119,021 unique GVKEY/date rows. For the actual-period match, dated SEC_IDHIST CUSIP and SEC_HISTORY primary-issue status are matched to dated CRSP names/PERMNO at DATADATE. The build finds 78,249 eligible dated primary-issue rows, with no ambiguous primary issue dates or PERMNO-date matches. It does not use header CUSIP or IID as historical identifiers.

Currency handling retains supported currencies. Same-currency estimates and accuracy comparisons stay in their common currency; a forecast revision expressed in different currencies is translated using the common event-date quote after share rebasing. Mixed-currency accuracy comparisons use the documented source-date rates. There are no missing event FX rates in the final sample. Its currency pairs are 4,204 USD/USD, 18 CAD/CAD, and one CAD/Compustat-USD event. For consensus, the latest eligible STATPERS date is selected first, then a same-currency row is preferred; when none exists, a unique other-currency snapshot is used. The diagnostics find 67 unique mixed-currency snapshots and no ambiguous latest snapshots.

## Repairs and bounded diagnostics

**Source-date split factors.** A1 used the event calendar's first-market-session-on-or-after map for per-share source dates as well. That can apply a split factor effective after a weekend/holiday source date. A2 keeps that map for event centers, return windows, and the t−2 event-price anchor, but now obtains source-basis factors from the last supported CRSP CFACSHR for the same PERMNO on or before each source date, with a maximum five-calendar-day gap. The map is applied separately to Detail ANNDATS (ACTDATS fallback only if missing), Summary STATPERS, and annual DATADATE. Rebased values use target/source factors. A Friday source date with CFACSHR 2, Sunday source date, and Monday split-effective factor 1 produced source factor 2 and rebased EPS/price of 50 from 100 in the smoke test; the event date and event-price anchor were preserved.

The production audit covers 3,358,283 linked Detail source rows: 16,314 have nontrading basis dates, and the former next-session factor would differ from the as-of factor for 11 rows, all on nontrading dates. Nineteen Detail source rows lack a factor within the five-day bound; the largest unsupported requested gap is 2,436 days. Among 383,240 history rows, 140,075 Compustat DATADATEs are nontrading, and 190 would receive a different next-session factor, all nontrading; Summary STATPERS has no such differences. Twenty-five history rows lack one or more bounded factors. These are input-row diagnostics, not counts of final affected events. The final event sample has no missing share factors. The audit also checks 7,152 adjacent nonunit factor changes and includes a synthetic 2-for-1 case; relative CFACSHR direction is target/source. Absolute Compustat AJEX and CRSP CFACSHR levels are not compared.

**Analyst and broker identities.** The I/B/E/S guide (2009, physical pp. 18, 52) describes ANALYS as a unique contributor identity and ESTIMATOR as the brokerage/estimator code. Accordingly, ability history is grouped by GVKEY and valid ANALYS; broker eligibility still counts ESTIMATOR. Of 3,463,076 Detail EPS rows, 218 have ANALYS=0, none are missing, and none are negative. Unassigned rows are not pooled as one analyst: they cannot receive ability scores and remain distinct unknown revisions in the broad confound pool. There are 65,057 GVKEY/ANALYS scoreable pairs; 5,085 span multiple estimator codes, covering 59,192 scoreable forecasts. Of 114,406 analyst-window candidates, 10,544 have scoreable history from another estimator and 6,685 span multiple estimator codes. The corrected analyst identity yields 50,965 ability-eligible windows versus 46,208 under the old broker-conditioned rule. This is a definition correction, not a sample-size target.

Revision sequencing retains the previous PERMNO. There are 696 ticker/analyst sequences with a PERMNO transition overall, 194 in FY1-primary sequences, and 250 in 1990–1994 across horizons. A revision crossing PERMNO is left unknown and is never calculated by dividing cumulative factors across issues. The source has no explicit identifier-continuity rule for such a transition; unknown transition revisions remain in the broad confound pool. The build records 920 unknown revisions after its guards.

**Other-revision confounds.** There are 638,183 distinct changed-revision signatures and no exact duplicate signatures. The broad pool intentionally includes all horizons and primary/diluted levels before the final target-event screens. For 50,965 main ability candidates, 22,518 have another same-analyst/time Detail row from another horizon (up to seven horizons). Each distinct changed row is treated as a possible separate announcement because the source says to exclude any other forecast-revision announcement but provides no event-level key for collapsing simultaneous horizon records. This is an explicit source-silent best-effort choice. The target's own revision is matched exactly once for every ability candidate; there are zero self-event mismatches.

Of those candidates, 45,946 overlap another revision; 7,307 overlap an earnings announcement; 5,351 overlap a dividend declaration; one lacks a complete return window; and two lack a t−2 price. Native Stata estimates pooled OLS with conventional covariance because the paper does not report clustering or a heteroskedasticity correction. The model has the source-specified common intercept and three mutually exclusive slope interactions. The actual estimation sample has 4,223 observations, F(3,4219)=7.27 (p=0.0001), R-squared=0.0051, and adjusted R-squared=0.0044. Slopes are 0.0866079 for superior (SE 0.0696004, p=0.213), 0.2384301 for normal (SE 0.0526957, p<0.001), and −0.0257985 for inferior (SE 0.1645531, p=0.875). The native pairwise tests are sup=norm F=3.10/p=0.0784, sup=inf F=0.40/p=0.5279, and norm=inf F=2.36/p=0.1249. Table exports round coefficients to three decimals and put p-values in parentheses. The actual-input descriptive export reports mean return 0.000297 (SD 0.029458) and mean winsorized revision −0.002634 (SD 0.010952); superior, normal, and inferior shares are 25.74%, 65.14%, and 9.12%.

Relative to A1's N=3,926, A2 has 297 more observations. Its slopes change from approximately 0.0819/0.2387/−0.0139 to 0.0866/0.2384/−0.0258; the pairwise p-values change from 0.0740/0.5948/0.1480 to 0.0784/0.5279/0.1249. The identity and basis corrections were made together, so these shifts cannot be attributed to one repair in isolation. Against the paper, A2 has 11,223 fewer observations; the superior and inferior estimates are far from their published slopes, while the normal slope is larger. Pairwise tests likewise differ, notably for superior versus inferior. The remaining gap is not explained by a single identified screen, and the available source data do not permit attributing it to a particular provider vintage or undisclosed implementation convention.

## Remaining limitations

- The source does not state whether its consensus statistic is mean or median. MEDEST is the available Summary History median and is used as a best-effort choice.
- The paper does not specify an actual-availability cutoff for ability scores. Requiring RDQ on or before the current event avoids using an actual not yet released but can reduce usable history.
- The paper does not define the market index/arithmetic for market-adjusted return or the standard-error correction. The implementation uses compounded CRSP value-weighted market return and conventional OLS covariance.
- The source does not specify whether all forecast horizons count as distinct “other” revision announcements, nor how to join simultaneous horizon records. The audit exposes this ambiguity and the implementation retains distinct changed rows.
- Nontrading event centers map to the next CRSP session; split-basis source dates use the as-of rule above. Equality-bin overlap at 20%/80% is resolved using the stated prose cutoffs.
- A2 remains a best-effort reconstruction: N=4,223 versus 15,446 in the paper. No additional sample or currency sensitivity runs were in scope.
